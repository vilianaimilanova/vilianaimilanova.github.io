I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Groningen, where I study how the framing of creative work — and who or what is credited for it — shapes how people perceive its value and their own role within it.

My current research examines how attributing creative output to an AI versus a human professional affects perceived task meaning, effort, and trust. I use cross-national survey experiments with representative samples in the United States and the Netherlands to test these questions at scale.

More broadly, I am interested in the social and behavioural dimensions of human–AI collaboration, organizational psychology, and the future of work.

Does It Matter Who Made It? AI Attribution and Perceptions of Task Meaning, Effort, and Trust

Viliana [Last Name] Working Paper

This paper examines how attributing a creative work product to an AI versus a human professional affects downstream evaluators' perceptions of task meaning, their own effort investment, and trust in the output. Using a pre-registered between-subjects survey experiment with two large, representative samples — UAS (United States, N ≈ 1,509) and LISS (Netherlands, N ≈ 2,116) — I find strong negative effects of AI attribution on trust that are robust across both national contexts. Effects on meaning and effort are null on average, though exploratory moderation analyses suggest that attitudes toward AI in the workplace condition the meaning response.
